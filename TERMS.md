## 1. What FlyFun Weather is

FlyFun Weather helps you plan for the weather on a flight. It relies on automated
analysis of third-party forecast and observation data and on AI, which can be wrong,
incomplete, out of date or unavailable, and it has not been reviewed by a
professional meteorologist.

- It is **one source among the others** you use to prepare a flight — the MET
  reports (METAR, TAF, SIGMET, AIRMET) and any other information you would normally
  check — and not a replacement for them.
- It is not professional meteorological advice.
- The **pilot in command remains solely responsible** for the decision to fly and
  for the safe conduct of every flight.

## 2. Flight-day and in-flight use

On the day of a flight the app shows observed data along the route — METARs,
SIGMETs, radar, storm cells, lightning, satellite cloud tops — the changes since the
briefing, and, if you start a flight in the app, your position on the route.

- Observed data reaches you **minutes old**, from sources that can be delayed,
  incomplete or unavailable, and it depends on a data connection you may not have
  in flight.
- Alerts and highlights can be **late, wrong or missing**. The absence of an alert
  does not mean the absence of a hazard.
- Positions and times derived from your phone's GPS and the flight plan are
  estimates.
- Nothing in the app is a means of avoiding weather in flight. It does not replace
  air traffic services, onboard weather equipment, or looking out of the window.
