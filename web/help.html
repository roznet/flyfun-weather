About WeatherBrief

WeatherBrief is an exploratory weather briefing tool for general aviation pilots. Its goal is to help you form an early picture of how weather patterns are coming together by comparing multiple numerical weather prediction (NWP) models. It exposes the rich data that forecasting models provide — soundings, cross-sections, thermodynamic indices, advisories — and lets you see where models agree and where they diverge, so you can gauge confidence days before a planned flight.

While it provides digests and advisories that distil the data into a simple green/amber/red system, the general philosophy is to expose all the raw data — soundings, cross-sections, thermodynamic indices, per-model values — with explanations, so you have full transparency into the underlying uncertainty and numbers behind any summary.

WeatherBrief is designed for longer-range planning, not for the final go/no-go decision. It does not include METARs, TAFs, SIGMETs, AIRMETs, or official weather synopses — for the day-of briefing you should always rely on those official sources from your national aviation authority.

Getting Started

  1. Create a flight on the Flights page by entering your waypoints (ICAO airport codes), date, time, altitude, and ceiling.
  2. Open the briefing by clicking the flight card. WeatherBrief will fetch forecast data from each enabled model, run thermodynamic analysis, compute advisories, and generate a synopsis.
  3. Refresh the briefing at any time to pull the latest available forecasts. Each refresh creates a new snapshot you can compare via the history dropdown.
You can configure which NWP models to use and tune advisory thresholds in Settings.

The Briefing Page

The briefing is organized into collapsible sections. Here's what each one shows:

Route Advisories

Advisories bring together the model data that informs key patterns a pilot may want to consider when making an early decision around flying, cancelling, or adjusting the timing of a flight. They look at conditions such as winds, icing, convection, cloud ceilings, and turbulence that can be deduced from the NWP models along your route.

Each advisory is rated GREEN, AMBER, or RED based on configurable thresholds. Advisories are evaluated per-model, so you can see whether models agree on a hazard or only one flags it. The summary rating shown for each advisory reflects the worst-case across all models.

Categories include icing, turbulence, cloud/visibility, convective activity, and airport conditions. You can enable, disable, and tune the thresholds of each advisory in Settings.

Synopsis

An LLM-generated plain-language overview of the weather situation along your route. It summarizes the key findings from the numerical analysis in a concise narrative. This section requires the AI Digest service to be enabled in Settings.

GRAMET Cross-Section

A vertical cross-section chart from autorouter.aero showing cloud, wind, temperature, and turbulence along your route. This requires the GRAMET service to be enabled and Autorouter credentials configured in Settings.

Cross-Section Visualization

An interactive canvas-based vertical cross-section built from the NWP model data. It renders multiple layers (clouds, icing, wind, humidity, etc.) that you can toggle on and off using the control panel.

  • Use the model selector to switch between forecast models.
  • Hover over the canvas to see values at a specific point.
  • Click to pin the readout at a location.
  • Toggle individual layers to isolate specific phenomena.

Skew-T Soundings

Skew-T / Log-P diagrams for each waypoint, generated from the model data. These show the vertical temperature and dewpoint profiles — essential for assessing stability, cloud bases, freezing levels, and convective potential. Use the model dropdown to compare soundings across models.

Route Profile

A slider that lets you move along the route and view a Skew-T sounding and key metrics at any interpolated point, not just the waypoints. Useful for examining conditions between airports.

Sounding Analysis

A table of thermodynamic indices derived from each sounding: CAPE, CIN, lifted index, freezing level, cloud layers, icing zones, and more. Values are shown per-model so you can spot where models agree or diverge.

Model Comparison

A divergence analysis that scores how well the models agree on key variables at each waypoint. High agreement gives confidence in the forecast; poor agreement signals uncertainty.

Info Buttons

Throughout the briefing you'll see small (i) buttons next to metrics and layer names. Clicking one opens a popup explaining what the metric measures, what the thresholds mean, and how to interpret the values in a flying context.

Settings

Services

  • Forecast Models — choose which NWP models are fetched on each briefing refresh. By default, Best Match, GFS, ECMWF IFS, and DWD ICON are enabled.
  • GRAMET — enable or disable Autorouter GRAMET cross-section charts.
  • AI Digest — enable or disable the LLM-generated weather synopsis.
  • Autorouter Credentials — enter your autorouter.aero username and password for GRAMET charts. Credentials are encrypted at rest.

Flight Parameters

  • Flight Defaults — set default cruise altitude and ceiling for new flights.
  • Advisory Models — select which models are used for advisory evaluation (must be a subset of your enabled forecast models).
  • Advisory Parameters — enable/disable individual advisories and fine-tune their thresholds (e.g. icing temperature band, crosswind limits).

Data Sources & Models

WeatherBrief fetches data from the following NWP models via Open-Meteo. Each model uses a seamless blend that automatically selects the highest-resolution variant available for the queried location.

Model Provider Coverage Resolution Range Levels Source
Best Match* Open-Meteo Global varies by location 16 days 25 Open-Meteo
GFS NOAA (US) Global 0.25° (~25 km) 16 days 25 Open-Meteo
ECMWF IFS ECMWF Global 0.25° (~25 km) 10 days 8 Open-Meteo
DWD ICON DWD (Germany) Global 11 km; 7 km over Europe 7 days 12 Open-Meteo
UK Met Office Met Office (UK) Global 10 km; 2 km over UK 7 days 17 Open-Meteo
Météo-France Météo-France Global 25 km; 11 km (EU); 2.5 km (France) 6 days 16 Open-Meteo
GEM ECCC (Canada) Global 15 km; 10 km (N. America) 10 days 17 Open-Meteo

*Best Match is Open-Meteo's automatic blend — it picks the highest-resolution model available at each location rather than running a single model.

Levels = number of pressure levels between 1000–300 hPa used for upper-air analysis (soundings, cross-sections). More levels give finer vertical detail. Range = maximum forecast horizon.
