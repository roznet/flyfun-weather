Help & Guide

About Flyfun Weather

Flyfun Weather is a weather briefing tool for general aviation pilots. It compares several numerical weather prediction (NWP) models along your route and translates them into aviation-focused advisories — icing, convection, cloud, turbulence, winds, airport conditions — rated green/amber/red so you can quickly see what matters for your flight.

Because it shows where the models agree and where they diverge, you can gauge how much to trust a forecast days before you fly. The briefing has two display modes: compact for the essentials, and full details, which exposes the numbers behind every summary — soundings, cross-sections, thermodynamic indices and per-model values, each with an explanation.

It follows a flight from the first look a week or more out, through the daily refreshes, to flight day — when it tracks the latest METARs, TAFs, SIGMETs, radar and lightning along your route — and on to the debrief afterwards.

Flyfun Weather is open-source and available on GitHub.

The iPhone & iPad App

Flyfun Weather has a companion app for iPhone and iPad. It signs in with the same account and shows the same briefings — the flights you create here appear there, and vice versa. It is built for three things the browser does poorly:

  • Reading on the phone. The briefing is laid out for a small screen rather than squeezed into one — advisories as cards you can thumb through, the cross-section and Skew-T drawn natively, and airport conditions side by side on iPad.
  • Taking the briefing with you. You can download a briefing before you leave, and it stays fully readable with no signal — including the cross-section and every sounding.
  • Flight day. The Observed tab shows what the weather is actually doing along your route. Press Start Flight and the app places you on the route from your phone's GPS, so ETAs, storms ahead and the destination TAF are measured against where you really are.

It also adds a few things that only make sense on a phone: a notification when a briefing finishes refreshing, and Siri shortcuts (“check my briefing”).

Install the iPhone & iPad app

Requires iOS or iPadOS 26.2 or later. Planning a flight and tuning your settings is still easiest here on the web.

Getting Started

Flyfun Weather is updated frequently with new features and refinements. Check the What's New tab to see the latest changes.

  1. Create a flight on the Flights page by entering your waypoints (airports, navaids, or fixes), date, time, altitude, and ceiling. Flights can be planned up to about 9 days ahead — the limit of the global model horizon. A flight saved further out waits and is briefed as soon as the models reach it.
  2. Open the briefing by clicking the flight card. Flyfun Weather fetches forecast data from each enabled model, runs the thermodynamic analysis, computes the advisories, and writes a synopsis.
  3. Refresh the briefing at any time to pull the latest available forecasts, or let the daily auto-refresh do it. Each refresh creates a new snapshot you can compare via the history dropdown.
  4. After the flight, record a quick debrief from the flight card: whether you flew or cancelled, and what weather mattered.
Several legs of one journey can be grouped into a trip, so you see whether the whole trip works, not just each leg. Set a Flexibility on a flight and the briefing also scans other departure times for you.
You can configure which NWP models to use and tune advisory thresholds in Settings.

The Briefing Page

The briefing has a left rail and a scrolling main pane. The rail holds the route, the Compact / Full Details toggle (compact is best for a daily glance, full for deep dives), how fresh each model is, the section navigation, and the history dropdown to re-open any past snapshot and see how the forecast has evolved. Every section has a focus button (⛶) to show it on its own.

Toolbar

The toolbar gives you Refresh, a Download menu (PDF briefing, or a MyGAR / Manifest6 file), Email, Feedback, the Tour (?-icon) and Share. Flight owners can also make a flight public or private (public flights get a shareable short link; others can subscribe to follow them read-only), set a daily auto-refresh hour, and choose when to be notified of a new briefing (by email or push) with the bell.

Overall Assessment

The banner at the top is the headline read on your flight — GREEN, AMBER, or RED — with a one-line reason. It comes from the AI synopsis, which weighs all the advisories together; without a synopsis it falls back to the worst-rated advisory. It's where to start before you dig into the detail below.

For flights more than about a week out — beyond the high-resolution model horizon — the banner instead shows a dashed Early outlook badge (Trending settled, Mixed signals, or Trending unsettled). This far ahead the models only agree on the broad pattern, so the briefing gives a confidence-led trend and tells you when more detailed analysis will become available.

Route Advisories

Advisories are the heart of the briefing. They translate raw NWP model data into aviation-relevant assessments that help you decide early whether to fly, cancel, or adjust the timing of a flight.

Each advisory is rated GREEN, AMBER, or RED and is evaluated per model, so you can see whether the models agree on a hazard or only one flags it. By default the summary rating is the majority of the models (a tie goes to the worse grade); you can switch to worst model in Settings for a more conservative read.

Advisory categories include:

  • Airport — forecast flight category, surface wind and crosswind against the runways, density altitude, low-level wind shear, and frost at departure.
  • Flight rules — VFR and IFR feasibility for the route, approach feasibility at the destination, and departure feasibility.
  • Icing — one icing advisory graded for your aircraft (with or without ice protection), plus freezing precipitation and, for aircraft without it, an icing escape check.
  • Cloud — cloud tops and whether you stay in VMC at cruise.
  • Convection — convective activity along the route and its character, from isolated or scattered showers to widespread, organised or embedded storms.
  • Precipitation, Turbulence & Winds — en-route precipitation, turbulence including mountain wind, and headwind/tailwind aloft.
  • Sun — low-sun glare down the runway on take-off or landing, and, for day-VFR flights, arriving close to sunset.
  • Fronts (experimental) — air-mass boundaries the models place across your route, when Auto Front Detection is on in Settings.

Use the altitude slider above the advisories to probe other levels and see instantly how each advisory shifts versus your planned altitude; the altitude table sweeps all levels at once and marks the best one. Where a different choice would improve a specific issue — a lower level, a later climb — a 💡 lightbulb opens options to improve. These are advice only and never change the grade.

You can enable, disable, and tune the thresholds of each advisory in Settings, as well as choose the analysis methods used for icing, cloud and convection.

Timing Scenarios

If the flight has a Flexibility set (alternate time, same day, or ±a day), the briefing re-grades the route at other departure times in the background and shows what would change if you left earlier or later.

Flight Day: Observed Conditions

On the day of the flight, the briefing adds an Observations group that follows what the weather is actually doing, updated every 10 minutes from about three hours before departure until after arrival:

  • At a glance — one line per phase of the flight, a comparison with what the briefing expected, departure and destination cards with runway and wind dials, and a short highlight of what matters now.
  • Along the route — a ribbon of rain, storm cells, SIGMETs and airports along the route, with the time you reach each.
  • Radar & lightning now — radar, lightning and satellite cloud tops around the route, with storm cells placed by side and distance off track, and whether they are closing or moving away.
  • Since this briefing — a banner of what has changed since the briefing was written, better or worse: a flight category, a new SIGMET, a storm near the route.

The observations annotate the briefing; they never re-grade it. After the flight, Along the route becomes a recap: what was seen where you actually were, against the forecast, with any moment of the flight replayable.

METAR/TAF & SIGMETs

As the flight approaches, a METAR/TAF section shows current observations and TAFs for your departure and arrival airports, fetched from official aviation weather sources, so you can cross-reference the model output with near-term observations. SIGMETs that intersect your route corridor (turbulence, icing, thunderstorms, mountain wave, etc.) are listed with their hazard type, altitude band, and validity.

Weather Alternates

As the flight gets close (about two days out), if your destination's forecast looks marginal, Flyfun Weather suggests nearby alternate airports that would fix the specific problem — a better flight category, lighter wind, or a kinder crosswind. Each candidate is shown as reachable before the destination (an early-decision diversion that avoids backtracking) or after it, with the extra distance each option costs. These are divert ideas for planning rather than an alternate-minima calculation. Turn them on with Weather alternates in your profile in Settings.

Is an Alternate Required?

With the alternates, the briefing also estimates whether the regulations would require you to file an alternate, computed two ways: FAA (14 CFR 91.169 — a binary Yes/No) and EASA Part-NCO (a Likely / Marginal / Unlikely confidence band). Because the published plate minima aren't known, the unknown value is expressed as a plausible range per approach type, which sets the width of the EASA band. Approaches not in the published data can be declared under Unpublished Approaches in Settings.

Synopsis

An AI-written plain-language overview of the weather along your route — the synoptic situation, specific concerns, the trend, and what to watch. It is written from the numerical analysis and the advisories. It requires the AI weather synopsis option in your profile in Settings; when it is off you can still generate one on demand from the banner.

Surface Pressure & Fronts

Surface analysis and forecast charts — fronts, pressure centres, isobars across Europe — from DWD, the Met Office and Météo-France (TEMSI), with your route drawn on top. Click any chart to enlarge.

DWD Synoptic Overview

The synoptic weather discussion from DWD (Deutscher Wetterdienst), giving a broader meteorological context for European flights.

▶ See it in a talk: The synoptic forecast — official front charts, and the air-mass boundaries inside each model.

Cross-Section

An interactive vertical cross-section built from the NWP model data. Layers are organised into groups (clouds, icing, turbulence, convection, stability, fronts, temperature lines, terrain, day and night) and you can toggle them individually in the control panel; presets show the layers behind each advisory.

  • Four layout modes — cross-section only, compare, side-by-side with the map, or map only — let you arrange the view to taste.
  • The model selector switches between forecast models; the compare layout overlays one layer across all models so you can see where they agree and where they diverge.
  • Switch theme (standard, light, high-contrast, GRAMET-style) and cloud rendering style (soft, natural puffs, square ForeFlight-like) to match your preference.
  • Hover shows values at the cursor; click pins a point and opens the matching Skew-T sounding below.
  • Multiple icing methods (Ogimet-DD, Ogimet-NWP, SFIP, IENG, SLD) and cloud methods (DD vs NWP) are available so you can compare how different physical assumptions affect the picture.

Route Graph

A graph below the cross-section, sharing the same x-axis (route distance). Pick any two metrics for the left and right Y-axes — wind speed, ceiling, CAPE, crosswind, temperature, and more — to see how a single quantity evolves along the route alongside the vertical layers above.

Route Map

An interactive map showing your route with segments colored by a selectable weather metric (wind, icing risk, cloud cover, humidity, etc.). Use the altitude slider to see how conditions change at different flight levels, and add overlays: radar, lightning, satellite, SIGMETs, fronts and airport forecasts.

▶ See it in a talk: the cross-section, route graph and map, comparing methods and models.

Skew-T Soundings

Skew-T / Log-P diagrams for any point on your route — click a point on the cross-section to load its sounding. Three view modes are available:

  • Dynamic — an interactive Skew-T with a side panel of ~14 variables (CAPE, CIN, lifted index, headwind/crosswind, cloud cover, etc.), overlay bands for cloud / icing / inversion / convective layers, and a linked cursor that highlights the same altitude on the cross-section.
  • Compare — overlays the sounding from every model on a single Skew-T so you can spot where they diverge in temperature, dewpoint, or parcel path.
  • Static — the classic MetPy-rendered image with CAPE/CIN shading, a hodograph, and an indices panel.

▶ See it in a talk: the Skew-T, the core of the onion.

Sounding Analysis

A table of thermodynamic indices derived from each sounding: CAPE, CIN, lifted index, freezing level, cloud layers, icing zones, and more. Values are shown per model so you can spot where models agree or diverge.

Model Comparison

A divergence analysis that scores how well the models agree on key variables at each waypoint. High agreement gives confidence in the forecast; poor agreement signals uncertainty.

Maps

The Maps page looks beyond a single route, across the airports Flyfun Weather monitors in Europe:

  • Forecast Overview — the forecast at each airport for any day and hour, per model or as the worst / majority across models. Useful to see which direction is flyable before you plan a flight.
  • Now — the latest METARs with radar, satellite infrared and lightning, refreshed every few minutes.
  • Synoptic Forecast (experimental) — fronts and air-mass boundaries from the models over the coming days.
  • Historical — replay the METARs, TAFs and model forecasts at any past moment.
  • Climatology — how often each airport has been flyable, by month, with a top-airports list.
  • Accuracy Stats — how the forecasts have verified against what was actually observed.

Info Buttons

Throughout the briefing you'll see small (i) buttons next to metrics and layer names. Clicking one opens a popup explaining what the metric measures, what the thresholds mean, and how to interpret the values in a flying context.

Settings

Aircraft & Flight Profiles

  • My Aircraft — your aircraft, with speeds and equipment (such as ice protection) that the advisories take into account.
  • Flight Profiles — separate settings for different aircraft or kinds of flight (e.g. VFR, IFR), each with its own defaults and advisory settings.
  • Flight Defaults — default cruise altitude, ceiling, speed, and flight-rules capability for new flights.
  • Forecast Models — choose which NWP models are used for each briefing.
  • Optional Services — the AI weather synopsis and weather alternates.
  • Advisories — the summary rating (majority or worst model), analysis methods for icing, cloud, and convection, front detection, and enabling or tuning each advisory (e.g. icing temperature band, crosswind limits).

Account

  • Language & Units — the display language for the app and the AI synopsis (English, French, German, Spanish), units (automatic by flight region, Europe or US), and currency.
  • Notifications — how you hear about refreshed briefings (email, push to the app).
  • Unpublished Approaches — approaches you can fly that aren't in the published data, used by the alternate checks.
  • MCP Access — API tokens for AI assistants (see below).
  • Usage & Your Data — your briefing usage, download all your data, or delete your account.

Data Sources & Models

Forecast Models

Flyfun Weather pulls each NWP model from multiple sources and blends them. Where available, raw GRIB2 from the originating forecast centre (ECMWF, DWD, NOAA, Météo-France, Met Office) provides the high-resolution upper-air sounding and cloud microphysics; outside those coverage areas (or beyond the direct horizon) Open-Meteo fills in. The table below is generated live from the server's source registry, so what you see is exactly what the briefing engine has available right now — including the most recent run, how far it covers, and when the next update is expected.

Full per-metric attribution lives in the Analysis Metrics Reference; the engine-level details are in Weather Engine Specs.

Open the full Data Sources tab → for every column we track (model family, resolution, cycles, latest run, covers until, next update).

Waypoint & Navigation Data

When you enter a waypoint that isn't an airport (a 5-letter fix, a navaid identifier, or a free-route point), Flyfun Weather looks it up in a local navigation database built from four free, public sources. The database is rebuilt on the 28-day AIRAC/NASR cycle and currently contains ~95,900 waypoints after deduplication.

Source Coverage Format Link
Eurocontrol FRA ~8,100 ECAC waypoints (FRA-significant 5LNC + NAVAIDs), per-AIRAC Excel .xlsx eurocontrol.int
OpenNav ~16,000 waypoints across 32 European countries (broader than FRA) HTML scrape opennav.com
OurAirports NAVAIDs ~9,900 worldwide NAVAIDs (VOR/DME/NDB/TACAN/VORTAC) CSV ourairports-data
FAA NASR Fixes ~70,200 US named fixes (intersections, RNAV, reporting points) Zipped CSV faa.gov NASR

All four sources are free, require no authentication, and refresh on the 28-day AIRAC/NASR cycle. When the same identifier appears in multiple sources, the deduplication priority is Eurocontrol FRA → OpenNav → OurAirports → FAA NASR — FRA wins ties because it carries the richest metadata (FIR codes, level availability).

AI Assistant Integration (MCP)

Flyfun Weather runs an MCP server, so you can work with your briefings from inside an AI assistant such as Claude. Instead of clicking through the site, you simply ask in plain language.

What you can do

  • Ask questions about a briefing. This is the highlight — interrogate the weather the way you'd ask a fellow pilot. “Why is the convective advisory red when it looks like blue sky?”, “What's the freezing level along the route?”, “Where do the models disagree?” The assistant drills into the per-model split, cross-checks, and synopsis to explain the reasoning, not just the headline colour.
  • Find the best day before you plan. “Is Saturday or Sunday better for Le Touquet?”, “Which direction is flyable from my base this weekend?” The assistant grades the airports around your base or along a route over the coming days, with a link to the forecast map for the slot it suggests.
  • Build it into your flight-planning workflow. Create a flight from a route, let the briefing generate automatically, then pull the weather assessment, advisories, and METAR/TAF — all in one conversation. “Create a flight LFMD to EGTF tomorrow morning and tell me how the weather looks.” You can also refresh a stale briefing when a newer model run lands, and on flight day ask what has changed since the briefing.

Every answer links back to the full interactive briefing here on the site for cross-sections, Skew-T diagrams, and route maps.

How to connect

FlyFun Weather is listed in Claude's connectors directory. In claude.ai or Claude Desktop:

  1. Open Customize → Connectors and press + next to Connectors to browse the directory. (From a chat: press +, hover Connectors, then Manage connectors.)
  2. Search for FlyFun Weather and press Connect.
  3. Sign in with your Flyfun Weather account and authorise access.

The assistant only ever sees your own flights and briefings. Once connected, the connector is also available in the Claude iOS and Android apps. If it isn't active in a conversation, press + in the chat, hover Connectors and switch FlyFun Weather on.

On a Claude Team or Enterprise plan, an organisation owner has to add FlyFun Weather under Organization settings → Connectors before members can connect it.

Using the MCP server directly

Any other MCP client can connect to the server at https://mcp.flyfun.aero/weather. Clients that support OAuth sign-in will prompt you to log in with your Flyfun Weather account; for tools that don't (such as Claude Code), generate an API token in Settings → MCP Access and supply it to the same URL.

ChatGPT

The FlyFun Weather custom GPT gets the same information as the MCP server above. As with Claude, you sign in with your Flyfun Weather account the first time, so it only ever sees your own flights and briefings.

Responsibility

Flyfun Weather combines automated analysis and AI to support your planning and decisions. Like any source it can occasionally be wrong or miss something, so use it alongside the other information you would normally check (METAR, TAF, SIGMET and the rest). The pilot in command remains responsible for the flight.
