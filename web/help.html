Help & Guide

About Flyfun Weather

Flyfun Weather is an exploratory weather briefing tool for general aviation pilots. Its goal is to help you form an early picture of how weather patterns are coming together by comparing multiple numerical weather prediction (NWP) models. At its core, the briefing translates model data into aviation-focused advisories — icing risk, convective potential, cloud ceilings, turbulence, crosswinds — rated green/amber/red so you can quickly see what matters for your route. An altitude advisory even suggests the best flight level based on the combined conditions.

Beyond the advisories, Flyfun Weather lets you see where models agree and where they diverge, so you can gauge forecast confidence days before a planned flight. The briefing offers two display modes: compact mode for a quick overview of the essentials, and full detail mode that exposes all the raw data — soundings, cross-sections, thermodynamic indices, per-model values — with explanations, giving you full transparency into the numbers behind any summary.

Flyfun Weather is designed primarily for longer-range planning — helping you build a picture of weather trends days before a flight. As the flight date approaches, it adds real-time METAR/TAF observations for a final cross-check, but you should always verify against official sources (SIGMETs, AIRMETs, national aviation authority briefings) before flying.

Flyfun Weather is open-source and available on GitHub.

Getting Started

  1. Create a flight on the Flights page by entering your waypoints (ICAO airport codes), date, time, altitude, and ceiling.
  2. Open the briefing by clicking the flight card. Flyfun Weather will fetch forecast data from each enabled model, run thermodynamic analysis, compute advisories, and generate a synopsis.
  3. Refresh the briefing at any time to pull the latest available forecasts. Each refresh creates a new snapshot you can compare via the history dropdown.
You can configure which NWP models to use and tune advisory thresholds in Settings.

The Briefing Page

The briefing is organized into collapsible sections. Here's what each one shows:

Route Advisories

Advisories are the heart of the briefing. They translate raw NWP model data into aviation-relevant assessments that help you make early decisions about flying, cancelling, or adjusting the timing of a flight.

Each advisory is rated GREEN, AMBER, or RED based on configurable thresholds. Advisories are evaluated per-model, so you can see whether models agree on a hazard or only one flags it. The summary rating shown for each advisory reflects the worst-case across all models.

Advisory categories include:

  • Icing — freezing level, icing intensity at cruise altitude, temperature/humidity overlap in the icing zone.
  • Convection — CAPE, lifted index, convective inhibition, and storm potential along the route.
  • Cloud & Visibility — cloud base height relative to your ceiling, coverage, and visibility conditions at departure and arrival.
  • Turbulence & Wind — vertical wind shear, crosswind components at departure and arrival airports, headwind/tailwind along the route.
  • Airport Conditions — surface wind, gusts, and crosswind at departure and arrival relative to available runways.
  • Altitude Advisory — analyses conditions at multiple flight levels and suggests the best altitude considering winds, icing, turbulence, and cloud avoidance.

You can enable, disable, and tune the thresholds of each advisory in Settings, as well as choose which analysis methods are used for icing and cloud detection.

METAR/TAF Observations

On the day of the flight, a METAR/TAF section is shown with current observations and TAF forecasts for your departure and arrival airports, fetched from official aviation weather sources. Useful for cross-referencing the NWP model output with real-time and near-term observations.

Synopsis

An LLM-generated plain-language overview of the weather situation along your route. It summarizes the key findings from the numerical analysis in a concise narrative. This section requires the AI Digest service to be enabled in Settings.

DWD Synoptic Overview

An optional synoptic weather discussion sourced from DWD (Deutscher Wetterdienst), providing a broader meteorological context for European flights.

GRAMET Cross-Section

A vertical cross-section chart from autorouter.aero showing cloud, wind, temperature, and turbulence along your route. This requires the GRAMET service to be enabled and Autorouter credentials configured in Settings.

Cross-Section Visualization

An interactive canvas-based vertical cross-section built from the NWP model data. It renders multiple layers (clouds, icing, wind, humidity, etc.) that you can toggle on and off using the control panel.

  • Use the model selector to switch between forecast models.
  • Hover over the canvas to see values at a specific point.
  • Click to pin the readout at a location.
  • Toggle individual layers to isolate specific phenomena.
  • Use the compare view to display a specific layer across all models side by side, so you can see how their forecasts overlap and where they diverge.

Route Map

An interactive map showing your route with segments colored by a selectable weather metric (wind, icing risk, cloud cover, humidity, etc.). Use the altitude slider to see how conditions change at different flight levels. Hover over a segment to see the value at that point.

Skew-T Soundings

Skew-T / Log-P diagrams for each waypoint, generated from the model data. These show the vertical temperature and dewpoint profiles — essential for assessing stability, cloud bases, freezing levels, and convective potential. Use the model dropdown to compare soundings across models.

Route Profile

A slider that lets you move along the route and view a Skew-T sounding and key metrics at any interpolated point (approximately every 10 km), not just the named waypoints. Useful for examining conditions between airports — for example, finding where a freezing level dips or where cloud cover thickens along a mountain crossing.

Sounding Analysis

A table of thermodynamic indices derived from each sounding: CAPE, CIN, lifted index, freezing level, cloud layers, icing zones, and more. Values are shown per-model so you can spot where models agree or diverge.

Model Comparison

A divergence analysis that scores how well the models agree on key variables at each waypoint. High agreement gives confidence in the forecast; poor agreement signals uncertainty.

Info Buttons

Throughout the briefing you'll see small (i) buttons next to metrics and layer names. Clicking one opens a popup explaining what the metric measures, what the thresholds mean, and how to interpret the values in a flying context.

Settings

Flight Profiles

  • Flight Defaults — set default cruise altitude, ceiling, speed, and flight-rules capability for new flights.
  • Forecast Models — choose which NWP models are fetched on each briefing refresh. By default, Best Match, GFS, ECMWF IFS, and DWD ICON are enabled.
  • Optional Services — enable or disable GRAMET cross-section charts and the AI Digest synopsis.
  • Advisories — configure summary rating aggregation (worst-case vs majority), analysis methods for icing, cloud, and convective advisories, enable/disable individual advisories, and fine-tune thresholds (e.g. icing temperature band, crosswind limits).
  • Advisory Models — select which models are used for advisory evaluation (must be a subset of your enabled forecast models).

Account

  • Language — choose the display language (English, French, German, Spanish).
  • Autorouter Credentials — enter your autorouter.aero username and password for GRAMET charts. Credentials are encrypted at rest.
  • Usage & Costs — view your briefing usage (this week, this month, and total) and per-briefing cost breakdown.

Data Sources & Models

Flyfun Weather fetches data from the following NWP models via Open-Meteo. Each model uses a seamless blend that automatically selects the highest-resolution variant available for the queried location. All metrics, calculations, and data sources are fully documented in the Analysis Metrics Reference.

Model Provider Coverage Resolution Range Levels Source
Best Match* Open-Meteo Global varies by location 16 days 28 Open-Meteo
GFS NOAA (US) Global 0.25° (~25 km) 16 days 28 Open-Meteo
ECMWF IFS ECMWF Global 0.25° (~25 km) 10 days 13 Open-Meteo
DWD ICON DWD (Germany) Global 11 km; 7 km over Europe 7 days 19 Open-Meteo
UK Met Office Met Office (UK) Global 10 km; 2 km over UK 7 days 20 Open-Meteo
Météo-France Météo-France Global 25 km; 11 km (EU); 2.5 km (France) 6 days 19 Open-Meteo
GEM ECCC (Canada) Global 15 km; 10 km (N. America) 10 days 20 Open-Meteo

*Best Match is Open-Meteo's automatic blend — it picks the highest-resolution model available at each location rather than running a single model.

Levels = number of pressure levels between 1000–150 hPa used for upper-air analysis (soundings, cross-sections). More levels give finer vertical detail. Range = maximum forecast horizon.
